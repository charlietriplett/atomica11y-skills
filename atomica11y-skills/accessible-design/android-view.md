# Design Android app view skills



## How to test an Android app view design

An Android app view must meet WCAG principles:

### 1. Perceivable

- Text can resize up to 200% with no loss of information - 1.4.4 AA
- Content is presented in a meaningful sequence - 1.3.2 A
- View can support pinch/zoom magnification - 1.4.4 AA
- Page view adapts to horizontal or vertical orientation - 1.3.4 AA

### 2. Operable

- View has a unique title heading that describes its purpose - 2.4.2 A
- The focus order is meaningful (even if it doesn't perfectly match the visual layout) - 2.4.3 A
- Offer at least two ways to find content (like navigation & search) - 2.4.5 AA
- Path based gestures (like slide, drag, drop) can be operated with single taps - 2.5.1 A

### 3. Understandable

- Help options are presented consistently in the page view. - 3.2.6 A

### 4. Robust

- Meets criteria across platforms, devices and viewports 4.0.0 AA

An Android app view must work for people with disabilities:

### Low vision

- Text can resize up to 200% with no loss of information - 1.4.4 AA
- View can support pinch/zoom magnification - 1.4.4 AA

### Cognitive

- Offer at least two ways to find content (like navigation & search) - 2.4.5 AA

### Motor

- The focus order is meaningful (even if it doesn't perfectly match the visual order)  - 2.4.3 A
- Page view adapts to horizontal or vertical orientation - 1.3.4 AA
- Path based gestures (like slide, drag, drop) can be operated with single taps - 2.5.1 A

### Blindness

- View has a unique title heading that describes its purpose - 2.4.2 A
- Content is presented in a meaningful sequence (even if it doesn't perfectly match the visual layout) - 1.3.2 A
- Meets criteria across platforms, devices and viewports 4.0.0 AA
